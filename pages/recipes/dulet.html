Meat & Spicy

Dulet

A boldly seasoned minced meat dish prepared with finely chopped meat, onion, garlic and hot Ethiopian spices.

⏱ 30–40 mins
👥 3–4 servings
← All Recipes

🛒 Ingredients

  • 1 lb lean beef, lamb or a mixture of lean meats, finely chopped
  • 1 large red onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp niter kibbeh or oil
  • 1 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tsp mitmita, optional for extra heat
  • 1 tbsp garlic, minced
  • ½ tsp ginger, minced
  • 1 small green chili, finely chopped
  • Salt to taste

🍳 Step-by-Step

  1. Clean and Prepare the Tripe: Wash the tripe thoroughly under cold running water. Dip the tripe briefly into a bowl of boiling water. Use the back of a knife to scrape away the dark, rough outer membrane until it is clean and white. Rinse one final time with cold water and a squeeze of lemon juice to neutralize any strong odors.
  2. Mince the Meats: Remove any tough membranes or connective tissues from the liver and kidneys. Using a very sharp knife, finely mince the tripe, liver, kidney, and lean meat into tiny, uniform cubes. Do not purée them in a food processor; they should retain a distinct, hand-chopped texture.
  3. Minutes 1–5 — Sauté Aromatics: Heat one tablespoon of cooking oil in a large skillet over medium-low heat. Add the finely diced red onions and garlic. Sauté for 5 minutes until soft and translucent, adding a pinch of salt.
  4. Minutes 5–20 — Simmer the Tripe: Add the minced tripe into the skillet along with 3 tablespoons of water or broth. Cover the pan with a lid, turn the heat to low, and let it simmer for 15 minutes. This braising step breaks down the tough fibers in the tripe, making it tender.
  5. Minutes 20–25 — Cook Lean Meat & Kidney: Take the lid off and turn the heat back up to medium. Stir in the minced lean meat and kidney. Sauté for 5 minutes until the moisture evaporates and the meat browns evenly.
  6. Minutes 25–28 — Flash Cook Liver: Toss in the minced liver. Sauté the whole mixture together for 3 minutes until the liver just changes color.
  7. Minutes 28–30 — Spice and Finish: Reduce the heat to low. Add the Niter Kibbeh (spiced butter), mitmita (or chili powder), ground cardamom, and black pepper. Stir thoroughly for 2 minutes to coat the meat evenly in the melting butter and spices. Toss in the chopped fresh green chilies during the last 30 seconds, then remove from the stove.
  8. Serve: Serve the Dulet hot right out of the pan alongside plenty of fresh injera. Eat traditionally by tearing off pieces of injera with your right hand and using it to scoop up the meat.
Kitchen note: Taste and adjust salt and spice gradually. Ethiopian dishes can vary by household and region, so the balance of heat, richness and acidity can be personalized.
