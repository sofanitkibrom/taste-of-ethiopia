Tihlo

A northern Ethiopian specialty made from barley dough shaped into small dumplings and eaten with a rich, spicy dipping sauce.

⏱ 50–70 mins
👥 3–4 servings
← All Recipes

🛒 Ingredients

  • 2 cups barley flour, plus extra as needed
  • Warm water, as needed
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 lb beef, finely chopped or minced
  • 2 red onions, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp oil or niter kibbeh
  • 2 tbsp berbere
  • 1 tbsp garlic
  • 1 tsp ginger
  • 2 cups water or stock
  • Salt to taste

🍳 Step-by-Step

  1. Prepare the Spicy Meat Sauce: Heat oil or niter kibe in a pan over medium heat. Add the chopped onions and cook until soft and golden brown. Add the minced garlic and ginger, cooking for another minute until fragrant. Stir in the berbere spice and cook for a few minutes, splashing a tiny bit of water so it does not burn. Add the blended tomatoes and let the mixture simmer until the oil separates from the sauce. Add your ground or finely chopped meat (or lentils) and stir well to coat with the berbere paste. Pour in enough water to create a thick, rich stew consistency. Cover and let it simmer on low heat until the meat is fully cooked and tender. Season with salt.
  2. Prepare the Barley Dough Balls: Place the roasted barley flour (besso) in a large bowl. Gradually add warm water and knead the mixture continuously with your hands until it forms a smooth, firm, and uniform dough that is pliable but not sticky. Pinch off small pieces of the dough and roll them between your palms into bite-sized round balls.
  3. Prepare the Hazo (Yogurt Sauce): Whisk together the plain yogurt, minced garlic, a pinch of ginger, salt, and a small pinch of the roasted barley flour until smooth.
  4. How to Serve and Eat: Place a central bowl of the hot, spicy meat sauce on a large platter or plate. Arrange the prepared barley dough balls neatly in a circle all around the central sauce bowl, often presented over a layer of flatbread or injera. Serve the hazo yogurt sauce in a small side bowl or dolloped on the side. Eat traditionally by using a small two-pronged wooden fork or skewer to spear a dough ball, dip it into the warm meat sauce, and optionally dip it into the cooling yogurt sauce before eating.
Kitchen note: Taste and adjust salt and spice gradually. Ethiopian dishes can vary by household and region, so the balance of heat, richness and acidity can be personalized.
