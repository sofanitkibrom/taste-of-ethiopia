Meat & Chicken

Doro Wat

A classic Ethiopian chicken stew built around slowly cooked onions, berbere and niter kibbeh, traditionally finished with hard-boiled eggs.

⏱ 60–75 mins
👥 4 servings
← All Recipes

🛒 Ingredients

  • 4 chicken thighs or drumsticks
  • 4 large red onions, very finely chopped
  • ¼ cup berbere spice blend
  • ¼ cup niter kibbeh or ghee
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tbsp garlic paste
  • 1 tbsp ginger paste
  • 1 tbsp tomato paste
  • ½ cup chicken stock or water
  • 4 hard-boiled eggs, peeled and gently scored
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Salt to taste

🍳 Step-by-Step

  1. Toss the chicken with lemon juice and a little salt. Let it rest while you prepare the stew base.
  2. Put the onions into a heavy pot over medium heat with no oil. Cook slowly for about 15 minutes, stirring often, until their moisture cooks away and the onions become soft and sweet.
  3. Add the oil and about half of the niter kibbeh. Stir in the garlic, ginger, berbere and tomato paste. Cook for several minutes, stirring constantly. Add a splash of water if the spice mixture starts sticking.
  4. Add the chicken and turn the pieces through the onion and spice mixture so they are well coated. Pour in the stock or water, cover, and simmer gently for about 20–30 minutes, turning the chicken once or twice, until it is cooked through and tender.
  5. Add the eggs and remaining niter kibbeh. Simmer uncovered for another 5 minutes so the sauce thickens and coats the chicken. Taste and adjust the salt.
  6. Serve hot over injera. A spoonful of ayib or a fresh salad can provide a cool contrast to the rich, spicy stew.
Kitchen note: Taste and adjust salt and spice gradually. Ethiopian dishes can vary by household and region, so the balance of heat, richness and acidity can be personalized.
